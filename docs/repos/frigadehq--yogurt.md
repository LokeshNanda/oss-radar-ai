## What it does
Yogurt is an AI-powered meeting notes application that records calls, transcribes speech to text, and generates summaries. It operates locally on your computer, ensuring that all data, including audio and transcripts, remains private.

## Why it's interesting
Unlike many other meeting assistants, Yogurt does not require a bot to join calls, which can be intrusive. It also emphasizes privacy by processing all data locally, making it a compelling alternative to cloud-based solutions like Granola.

## How it works
Yogurt captures audio from your microphone and system sound, transcribing it using Whisper and summarizing it with a local AI model. It supports multiple languages and integrates with popular video conferencing tools like Zoom, Teams, and Meet, although speaker identification is limited to Mac users. The application uses Electron for its interface and requires a one-time download of AI models for functionality.

## Get started in 5 minutes
To try Yogurt, download the appropriate installer for your OS from the releases section on GitHub. After installation, follow the setup instructions to download the necessary AI models, which will allow you to start recording and transcribing meetings.

## Watch out for
Yogurt is still relatively new, having been created in November 2026, and may have undiscovered bugs or limitations. It requires at least 8 GB of RAM and specific OS versions (macOS 14.4+, Windows 10 2004+, Ubuntu 22.04+) to function properly. Additionally, while it is open-source under the MIT license, users should be aware of the potential security implications of allowing other applications to access their notes through the MCP server feature.
